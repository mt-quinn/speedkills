What am I looking at?

Ships. Icons point where the ship points; the bar under each is its hull. Faded = behind the planet. Arrows at the screen edge point to ships off screen.
Orbit. Where the ship will coast if it doesn't burn.
Missiles. Bright while the motor burns (seen from anywhere), dim once it's out (dark to its target beyond 300 m). The trail is where it has been; a red dashed line means its seeker has locked on.
Breaking. Strafing can't escape a missile. A hard main-engine turn across its path can, if it's timed early enough, and how early depends on how agile that missile is. Early breaks are safe but burn a lot of fuel.
Incoming. A round its target can see, predicted to pass close; the target shows a countdown.
Dark rounds. Rounds are invisible to their target until within 300 m. Hollow dots are rounds the target can't see yet; "UNSEEN ROUND" means one is on target and only you know.
Sensing. Ships see each other within 300 m (the faint ring), or at any range when the other is loud: engine lit, firing, glowing in the air or just hit (pulsing rings). The planet blocks the view. Strafing is quiet.
Their guess. Out of sight, a ship only knows where the enemy would be if it had coasted: the hollow diamond, inside a dashed ring of how far it could have moved unseen. A dotted line shows how wrong the guess is.
Safe zone. Beyond it, amber and red bands burn hull. From 2:30 it shrinks.
Atmosphere. Dipping in brakes the ship and heats the hull. The planet itself is lethal.
Skipping. Nose into the airflow, belly to the planet, a ship skips back out and scoops propellant as it goes (the glow turns blue-white, the fuel bar pulses). Broadside or belly-up, it digs in and crashes.

Broadcast. The Director camera runs a few seconds behind live. That lets it see the big moments coming: it slows down and zooms in for them, draws what happened (the 15 m fuze ring, how far the round passed, where a dodging ship would have been), replays kills from behind the killing round, and fast-forwards quiet stretches. The badge under the clock says what time is doing.

Missiles burst within 8 m of a hull, for less damage the farther out. Damage is the warhead, not the closing speed, so a shot into an oncoming orbit hits hardest. Every dodge costs propellant; the only way to get more is to skip off the air.
